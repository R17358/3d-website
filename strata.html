TRIASSIC — 252–201 MYA

Dinosaurs start small, after everything else nearly ended.

This period opens right after Earth's worst extinction ever wiped out most life on the planet. Into that empty world walk the first dinosaurs — fast, bipedal, mostly no bigger than a dog. Herrerasaurus and Eoraptor are among the earliest known.

JURASSIC — 201–145 MYA

Pangaea splits, and the giants show up.

Wetter climates and breaking continents open room to grow. Sauropods like Brachiosaurus and Diplodocus become some of the largest land animals ever, armored Stegosaurus appears, and predators like Allosaurus scale up to match.

CRETACEOUS — 145–66 MYA

The most diverse period dinosaurs ever have.

Flowering plants spread across the planet for the first time, and dinosaur diversity peaks alongside them. This is the world of Tyrannosaurus, Triceratops, and hundreds of other named species — more than either period before it.

K–PG BOUNDARY — 66 MYA

Then, in geological terms, it ends in an afternoon.

An asteroid roughly 10km wide strikes near what is now the Yucatán Peninsula. The impact and its aftermath end the reign of every non-avian dinosaur — about 75% of all species on Earth — and leave a thin layer of iridium-rich clay found worldwide.
